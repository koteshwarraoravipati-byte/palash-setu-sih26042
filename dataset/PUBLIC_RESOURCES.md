# Public Santhali resource register

This file records sources investigated for the PALASH Setu prototype. A public dataset is not automatically a correct classroom translation. Every lesson phrase must still be reviewed by a Santhali-speaking teacher or language expert.

## Common Voice Santali (Ol Chiki)

Source: https://mozilladatacollective.com/datasets/cmqie985k00cbnr07z9cea5wy

- Dataset: Common Voice Scripted Speech 26.0 — Santali (Ol Chiki)
- Language code: `sat`
- Reported contents: 854 clips, 533 validated clips, approximately 0.66 validated hours, 14 speakers
- License shown on the source page: CC0-1.0
- Appropriate uses: speech research, ASR benchmarking, computer-aided language learning research, and language revitalisation
- Important restrictions shown on the source page: do not attempt to identify speakers; do not re-host or re-share the dataset

The complete dataset must not be copied into this repository, bundled into the APK, or redistributed from GitHub. Use the official source page and its permitted access method. Individual classroom audio should be recorded separately with informed permission and reviewed for child-friendly pronunciation.

## BhashaVerse / LTRC

Source: https://ltrc.github.io/news

LTRC describes BhashaVerse as a free-to-download collection of multilingual resources and models supporting Santhali among other Indian languages. The exact license and redistribution terms for any model or data artifact must be confirmed from the release package or directly with LTRC before bundling it in a Play Store app.

Until that verification is complete, PALASH Setu must not ship an LTRC model or dataset inside the app.

## PALASH multilingual education context

Source: http://www.mle-india.net/2024/10/palash-multilingual-education-program.html

Use the PALASH/MTB-MLE context to align lesson design and deployment assumptions. It is not a license for copying third-party lesson text, illustrations, or audio.

## Approved-content rule

A row may be marked `approved` only when:

1. the Hindi source is appropriate for the intended grade and FLN outcome;
2. a Santhali-speaking reviewer approves the target text and script;
3. a second reviewer or back-translation check is completed where practical;
4. the audio is recorded with permission and reviewed for pronunciation;
5. provenance, license, reviewer role, date, and pack version are recorded.

The app must display `Review required` for all other rows and must never invent a target-language phrase.
